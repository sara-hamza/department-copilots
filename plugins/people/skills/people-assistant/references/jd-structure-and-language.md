# Job description structure and language

A good JD tells a capable person what the job is for, what they will actually do, what they truly need, and why they would want it. It does not read like a wish list or a compliance form.

## Intake questions (ask when the input is thin)

1. What are the three outcomes this role must deliver in its first year?
2. Who does the role work with daily (team, other departments, RSPs, contractors, developers, customers)?
3. What would make someone fail in this role?
4. Is this a replacement or a new role, and what has changed since the last JD?
5. Level, reporting line, location, hours, on-call or site work, and whether hybrid applies.

## Section order

| Section | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Role summary | Why the role exists and what success looks like | 3 to 4 sentences |
| What you will do | Responsibilities as outcomes, not tasks | 6 to 10 bullets |
| What you need (essential) | Only what excludes someone who could do the job | 4 to 7 bullets |
| Nice to have | Helpful, genuinely optional | 2 to 5 bullets |
| How we work | Hours, on-call, travel, site, hybrid, licences, physical requirements | Short, factual |
| About the team | Who they join, scale, what the team is working on | 2 to 4 sentences |
| Process and contact | How to apply, who to contact, closing date | Short |

Leave salary, reporting line and location as `[CONFIRM: ...]` placeholders if not supplied. Do not invent them.

## The requirements test

For each line under essential requirements, ask:

1. Would a candidate who could do this job well be excluded by this line? If yes, it is not essential.
2. Is it a proxy for something else? "Degree in telecommunications" is often a proxy for "understands optical networks and can read a design". Write the outcome, not the proxy.
3. Is the number arbitrary? "Five years' experience" excludes people with four excellent years and admits people with ten poor ones. Prefer "experience operating a live GPON or carrier network" and let the interview test depth.
4. Can it be learned in the first three months with normal support? Then it is nice to have.
5. Is it a certification the law or a customer actually requires (for example a White Card for construction sites, an electrical licence, a driver's licence for a field role)? Then it is essential and should say why.

## Outcome-led responsibility wording

| Task wording (weak) | Outcome wording (better) |
|---|---|
| Monitor the network | Keep service-affecting faults visible and actioned within the NOC response targets |
| Attend meetings with developers | Represent the company's technical requirements to developers so that estate designs are approved first time |
| Complete reports | Give delivery leads a weekly view of progress, blockers and risks they can act on |
| Manage contractors | Ensure contractor work meets the construction standard and defects are closed before practical completion |
| Handle tickets | Resolve RSP fault tickets within SLA with evidence-led communication |

## Inclusive language table

| Avoid | Prefer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rockstar, ninja, guru, wizard | Experienced, skilled, specialist | Gendered and age-coded in practice; vague |
| Young, energetic, digital native, recent graduate | Early-career (if the level is early-career), or drop it | Age discrimination risk |
| Mature, seasoned | Experienced | Age-coded |
| Aggressive, dominant, competitive | Decisive, confident, persistent | Masculine-coded, deters applicants |
| Nurturing, bubbly | Supportive, collaborative, approachable | Feminine-coded |
| Native English speaker | Clear written and spoken English | Nationality discrimination risk |
| Must be able to lift 20 kg | State the actual physical requirement and that adjustments are available | Only include if genuinely required |
| Culture fit | Culture add, works well with the team's ways of working (then describe them) | "Fit" screens out difference |
| He / she, his / her | They / their, or "you" | Neutral |
| Work hard play hard, fast-paced, high pressure | Describe the actual rhythm: on-call roster, release cycle, peak periods | Signals burnout, deters carers |
| Manpower, chairman, tradesman | Workforce, chair, tradesperson | Gendered nouns |

Also:
- Use "you" and "we". Avoid "the successful candidate will".
- State flexibility honestly. "Flexible working available" means nothing without a sentence on how it works in this team.
- Say adjustments are available for the recruitment process and the role, and name a contact.
- Keep reading level plain: short sentences, no internal acronyms without expansion (ONT, OLT, FDH, RSP, NNI).

## Role family notes for the company

| Role family | Typical essentials | Common nice-to-haves |
|---|---|---|
| NOC Engineer | Operating a live carrier or ISP network, GPON or FTTx fault isolation, shift and on-call availability, clear ticket writing | Vendor OLT experience, scripting, RSP-facing experience |
| Network Engineer | Routing and switching design and operation (IGP, BGP, MPLS or EVPN), change management, config review, vendor CLI | Automation, lab tooling, GPON OLT platforms |
| Field Technician | Fibre splicing and testing (OTDR, power meter), safe work on sites, driver's licence, White Card where required | FTTP premises installation, ONT commissioning, estate experience |
| Design Engineer | FTTP or outside-plant design, CAD or GIS tools, reading construction drawings, BOM preparation | Estate planning cycles, council and utility processes |
| Construction Supervisor | Civil and fibre construction supervision, contractor management, QA and defect closure, WHS | Pit and pipe standards, as-built review |
| Project Coordinator / Manager | Schedule, RAID and stakeholder management, status reporting, developer or council liaison | Telco delivery, estate handover processes |
| Customer Operations | Complaint handling, plain-English writing, working with wholesale partners | Telco consumer protection frameworks |
| BD / Developer Relations | Account development with property developers, proposal writing, commercial negotiation | Greenfield telco, planning and staging knowledge |
| Corporate (Finance, Procurement, People, Marketing) | Function-specific essentials; state the systems honestly as "experience with similar tools" rather than naming one product | Telco or infrastructure sector exposure |

## Review checklist before publishing

1. First sentence says what the role is for.
2. Every essential requirement passes the requirements test.
3. No coded or gendered language (table above).
4. Hours, on-call, site work and travel are stated honestly.
5. Adjustments statement and contact included.
6. Placeholders `[CONFIRM: ...]` resolved by the hiring manager.
7. Australian English, no em-dashes, acronyms expanded on first use.
