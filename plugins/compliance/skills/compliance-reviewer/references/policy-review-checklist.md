# Policy and procedure review checklist

Use for internal policies, procedures, standards and work instructions. The goal is a document that people can follow, that someone owns, that aligns with the obligations it is meant to meet, and that can be shown to be followed.

This is not legal advice. Outputs are for review by qualified staff.

## 1. Document control

| Check | Finding if absent |
|---|---|
| Title, document ID, version, effective date, review date | Cannot tell if current |
| Owner (role) and approver | No one accountable |
| Change history | Cannot see what changed |
| Classification (internal, confidential) | Unclear who may see it |
| Related documents and the one that governs on conflict | Conflicting instructions |

## 2. Purpose and scope

- Purpose stated in one or two sentences
- Who it applies to: all staff, contractors, specific teams, RSPs, developers
- What it covers and what it does not
- Definitions for every term that could be read two ways

## 3. Alignment with obligations

- Which external obligations the policy implements (legislation, licence conditions, industry codes, contracts, standards): are they named and current?
- Does each obligation have a corresponding control or step in the policy?
- Does the policy promise more than the obligation requires (creating a self-imposed standard that can be breached)?
- Does it conflict with another internal policy? Name both and the clause.

## 4. Roles and responsibilities

- Every "must" has a role attached
- Approvals: who approves what, with thresholds
- Escalation path and timeframes
- Delegations when the named role is unavailable
- Responsibilities placed on contractors or partners: are they flowed down through a contract?

## 5. The procedure itself

- Steps in order, each with an actor and an output
- Decision points with clear criteria (not "use judgement" alone)
- Timeframes that are measurable (hours, business days) and achievable with current resourcing
- Records to keep, where, for how long
- Exceptions process: who can grant, how recorded
- Interfaces: what triggers the procedure and what it hands over to

## 6. Evidence and assurance

- What evidence shows the policy was followed (forms, tickets, logs, sign-offs)
- Monitoring and reporting: who checks, how often, to whom
- Internal audit or review cycle
- Breach handling: how non-compliance is reported, investigated and recorded; whether reporting is protected

## 7. Training and communication

- Who must be trained, when (on start, on change, annually), and how it is recorded
- How the policy is published and how changes are communicated
- Acknowledgement requirements

## 8. Usability

- Reading level suitable for the audience; plain language; examples for the hard parts
- Length proportionate to the risk
- Forms and templates referenced actually exist and match the procedure
- Consistent terms (one name per thing)
- No em-dashes, no double negatives, Australian English

## Common policy types and the specific checks

| Policy | Specific checks |
|---|---|
| Privacy and data handling | Collection purposes, notice, access and correction, security, retention and destruction, breach assessment and notification steps and timeframes, overseas disclosure, roles |
| Information security | Access control, acceptable use, passwords and MFA, device and remote working rules, incident reporting path, logging, vendor access, review cycle |
| WHS | Hazard identification, risk assessment, site rules, PPE, incident reporting and investigation, consultation, contractor management, training records |
| Code of conduct | Expected behaviour, conflicts of interest, gifts, confidentiality, use of company resources, reporting and consequences, protection for reporters |
| Procurement and delegations | Approval thresholds, competitive quote requirements, supplier due diligence, conflict declarations, contract signing authority |
| Complaints and customer handling | Timeframes, escalation, record keeping, wholesale versus retail responsibility, external dispute routes |
| Change and network operations | Change categories, approval, maintenance windows, notification to RSPs, rollback, post-implementation review |
| Business continuity | Critical services, recovery objectives, roles, communication tree, testing frequency |
| Records management | What is a record, retention schedule, storage, destruction, legal hold |

## Output for a policy review

Use the COMPLIANCE REVIEW template with these interpretations:

- OBLIGATIONS: what the policy requires of staff or teams, and the external obligations it implements
- RISKS: gaps between obligation and control, unachievable timeframes, missing roles, conflicts with other policies
- DATES: effective, review, training and reporting cycles
- RESPONSIBILITIES: the roles table
- AMBIGUITIES: steps with two readings
- MISSING INFORMATION: referenced forms, standards, related policies not supplied
- QUESTIONS FOR LEGAL: where the policy may under- or over-state a legal obligation
