# Audience matrix

What each audience cares about, the words they use, what to lead with, what to leave out, what they ask first, and the headline angles that work. Facts stay identical across audiences; emphasis and vocabulary change.

## Core matrix

| Audience | Cares about | Vocabulary | Lead with | Leave out | Channel norms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Residents (new and existing) | Can I connect, with which provider, when, what it costs (from the provider), what to do on move-in day, who to call when it does not work | "fibre to your home", "connection box", "your provider", "your estate" | Whether and when they can connect, and the next step | Acronyms, internal process, network architecture, developer obligations | Email, estate letter, web page, social, SMS (short), welcome pack |
| Developers | Programme certainty, responsibilities, what they can tell buyers, minimal rework, a single contact | "RFS", "stages", "civil handover", "scope", "lead-in" | Dates and what each party does | Resident-level hand-holding, network jargon beyond what they need | Email, proposal, programme update, site meeting note |
| RSPs | Service availability, provisioning process, timing, technical parameters, incident and change notices | "RFS", "ONT", "UNI", "NNI", "service qualification", "order", "change window" | The service and process impact, precisely | Marketing language, resident emotion | Email notice, partner portal text, technical bulletin |
| Internal staff | What it means for their work, what to say and not say externally, who owns it | Plain internal language | What changes for them and the external line | Nothing hidden; but no customer personal data | Intranet note, Teams post, team email |
| Media and community | The news, the place, who benefits, numbers, a quote | Plain, local | The announcement and local relevance | Technical detail, commercial terms | Media release, community newsletter, council update |

## What each audience asks first (use for FAQs)

| Audience | First questions |
|---|---|
| Residents | Is my home connected or ready? Which providers can I choose? How do I get connected? When? What does it cost? Do I need a technician visit? What is the box on my wall? Who do I call if it stops working? Can I keep my current provider? Will it work with my modem? |
| Developers | When is each stage ready for service? What do we need to build and to what specification? Who arranges power and the cabinet site? What can we tell buyers? What happens if civil is late? Who is our contact? |
| RSPs | When is the estate RFS? How many premises and lot types? What is the service qualification process? Which speed tiers and products are available? What are the ONT and UNI details? What is the provisioning lead time? Where do incidents and changes get notified? |
| Internal | What is being announced and when? What do I say if a resident or developer asks? Who owns questions? What is not public yet? |
| Media and community | What is it? Where? How many homes? When? Who benefits? Who can be quoted? |

## Headline angles by audience

| Angle | Residents | Developers | RSPs | Internal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benefit | "Fibre to your home is ready at <estate>" | "Stage 2 ready for service on schedule" | "<Estate> Stage 2 RFS: 180 premises available to order" | "<Estate> goes live: what to tell callers" |
| Proof or specific | "<n> homes at <estate> can now connect" | "<n> premises delivered <x> weeks ahead of settlement" | "<n> premises, <speed tiers>, ordering open <date>" | "<n> premises live, <n> RSPs onboarded" |
| News | "Fibre arrives at <estate> this <month>" | "<Estate> fibre network declared ready for service" | "RFS notice: <estate> Stage 2" | "Announcement today: <estate> RFS" |
| Question | "Moving to <estate>? Here is how to get connected" | "Is your next stage fibre-ready?" | (rarely used) | "Had a question about <estate>? Start here" |
| Local | "<Suburb>'s newest community is connected" | "<Estate> joins <n> connected communities in <region>" | (rarely used) | (rarely used) |
| Urgency or action | "Order before <date> to be connected at move-in" (only with a real date) | "Civil handover by <date> keeps Stage 3 on track" | "Order window opens <date>" | "Brief your team before <date>" |

Use urgency only when the input gives a real date. Never manufacture scarcity.

| Media release headline | 80 characters |

## Resident copy rules (wholesale model)

- Never imply residents buy from the network operator. "Choose a provider on our network."
- Never quote plan prices or speeds unless the provider supplied and approved them.
- Explain the two-party model once, simply: "the network operator builds and runs the fibre network in your estate. Internet providers sell plans over it. You choose a provider; they set up your service."
- Fault wording: "If your service stops working, contact your provider first. They will work with us if the network needs attention."
- Technician visits: say when one is needed and who books it (the provider, after an order).

## Converting one piece into versions

1. Extract the FACT LIST.
2. For each audience, pick the lead from the matrix, rewrite in their vocabulary, drop what the matrix says to leave out.
3. Check each version against the fact list; flag any fact that had to be omitted for a channel limit.
4. Write the WHAT CHANGED table so the author can see the differences at a glance.
