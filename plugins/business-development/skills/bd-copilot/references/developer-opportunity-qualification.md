# Developer opportunity qualification

Qualify before proposing. Each field has a question to ask, a signal that the opportunity is strong, and a signal that it is weak or premature. Missing fields become QUESTIONS TO ASK.

## Qualification table

| Field | Ask | Strong signal | Weak or premature signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lot count and dwelling mix | How many lots or dwellings in total, and by stage? Detached, townhouse, apartments, mixed use, retirement? | Above the minimum viable size (CUSTOMISE: 100 lots default) or part of a larger masterplan; mix is known | Under 50 lots with no follow-on stages; mix undecided |
| Staging | How many stages, in what order, and what is the civil start and title registration date for each? | Stage plan exists with dates; Stage 1 civil within 6 to 12 months | No staging plan; "depends on sales" |
| Timing | When does civil start, when is practical completion, when are first settlements? | Dates in a programme; first settlement 12 months or more away (enough for design, build, RFS) | First settlements inside 6 months with no civil started; or no dates at all |
| Civil scope and standard | Who designs and builds pit and pipe? To which specification? Is there a civil contractor appointed? | Developer will build pit and pipe to our specification (CUSTOMISE spec name) or wants us to; contractor known | Pit and pipe already built to another operator's standard; contractor unknown |
| Comms room or FDH site, power | Is there a site for the FDH or active equipment with power, access and tenure? | Site identified on the plan, power arranged, access agreed | No site, or site in a future stage |
| Competing networks | Has NBN Co, another private operator or the local council been approached? Is there an existing agreement or a notice of intention to build? | No competing commitment; developer open; or dissatisfied with the incumbent on a previous estate | Agreement already signed elsewhere; NBN Co works already under way |
| Contract status | Is there an agreement in place, draft, or none? What term and conditions does the developer expect? | No agreement yet but decision in the next quarter; developer has used our agreement before | Legal review stalled; developer expects terms we cannot meet (flag to commercial owner) |
| Decision-maker | Who signs? Who influences (development manager, civil consultant, planner, sales)? | Direct access to the signer; development manager champions | Only a consultant contact; signer unknown |
| Planning status | Is the development approved? Are titles expected on schedule? | Approved with conditions met | Pre-approval; appeals pending |
| Resident service expectations | What has the developer promised buyers (speed, providers, move-in readiness)? | Promises match what we deliver | Promises we cannot meet (named RSP not onboarded, speed not offered) |
| Developer history | Have they built with us before? Any issues on previous estates? | Repeat developer, good outcomes | Previous dispute or defect history (handle, do not avoid) |

## Rating the opportunity

| Rating | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | Lot count above minimum, staging with dates, no competing commitment, civil and comms room workable, decision-maker reachable, decision within two quarters |
| Moderate | Most fields positive; one material gap (timing tight, comms room unresolved, decision-maker unclear) with a path to close it |
| Weak | Competing commitment signed, lots below minimum with no follow-on, or timing that cannot be met |
| Insufficient information | Fewer than half the fields known; brief lists the questions instead of a pitch |

State the rating with the fields that drove it. Do not round up.

## Priorities by developer type (inferred, label as such)

| Developer type | Usual priorities |
|---|---|
| Large masterplan developer | Programme certainty across many stages, single point of contact, consistent resident experience, marketing support for "fibre-ready" claims, competitive commercial terms over a long term |
| Mid-size land developer | Settlement dates met, minimal civil rework, clear scope split, simple agreement |
| Builder-developer (house and land) | Service live at handover to the buyer, no delays at move-in, simple process for their site supervisors |
| Apartment or MDU developer | In-building design early (risers, comms room), coordination with electrical and fire trades, timing with occupancy certificate |
| Retirement or lifestyle village operator | Reliability, support for less technical residents, bulk or community services, long-term operator relationship |
| Government or community housing | Compliance, procurement process, reporting, equity of service |

## Timing rules of thumb (CUSTOMISE)

- Design: 6 to 10 weeks after civil drawings are final.
- Civil (developer): depends on the stage; we need pit and pipe complete and accepted before haul.
- Haul, splice, FDH and active equipment: 8 to 14 weeks after civil handover, stage dependent.
- RFS to first connection: RSP onboarding must be complete before RFS; allow 4 weeks for RSPs not already on the estate.
- Rule: first settlement date minus 6 months is the latest comfortable civil handover for that stage. Earlier is better.

## Red flags to raise in the brief

- Pit and pipe already built to a different specification (rework cost and ownership).
- Comms room in a stage that completes after the first settlements.
- Developer has promised a named RSP that is not on our network.
- Agreement expected to include terms outside policy (exclusivity, revenue share, build timing penalties) without commercial owner involvement.
- Decision-maker unknown after two meetings.

## CUSTOMISE

- Minimum viable lot count and any exceptions (strategic location, adjacent to existing network).
- Civil specification name and version the developer must build to.
- Standard agreement name and the commercial owner who approves deviations.
- RSPs currently available on new estates.
- Typical lead times above; replace with the delivery team's current figures.
