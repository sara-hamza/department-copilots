# Objection handling

Each objection has the real concern behind it, a response outline, and the evidence to bring. Respond on our strengths; never disparage the alternative. Use the writing-helper developer tone: professional, confident, outcome-focused.

## Objection table

| Objection as the developer says it | Real concern | Response outline | Evidence to bring |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Why not just use NBN?" | Risk of choosing the non-default; perceived resident expectation | Acknowledge NBN is the default many developers know. Explain what a private FTTP operator does differently for a greenfield estate: a single accountable party for design, build and operation; programme aligned to their stages; RSP choice on an open-access network; a direct relationship for defects and resident issues. Then ask what matters most to them and go to that. | Delivered estates list with stage timing met (CUSTOMISE); RSP list; resident support model |
| "What does it cost us?" | Capital outlay and who pays for pit and pipe | Be clear about the scope split: what the developer builds (civil to spec, as they would for any operator) and what the company funds. State that commercial terms are set out in the proposal; do not quote figures in the room unless authorised. | Scope split table; agreement summary; [PRICING placeholder] |
| "Your timing will not meet our settlements" | Programme risk; they have been burned before | Walk the milestone chain: civil handover -> haul -> RFS. Show the lead times and the latest comfortable civil handover for their first settlement. Offer the staged approach so Stage 1 is live while later stages are built. | Lead-time table (CUSTOMISE); example programme from a comparable estate |
| "Buyers want to choose their provider" | Lock-in fear | Open-access wholesale: residents choose among the RSPs on the network; list them. If a specific RSP is not present, say so honestly and what onboarding involves. | Current RSP list; onboarding timeline |
| "We have already built pit and pipe" | Rework cost | Ask for the as-builts and the specification used. Many designs can be used with minor changes; where not, scope the delta clearly rather than reject. | Civil specification; a review process for existing works |
| "We signed with someone else on the last estate and it was fine" | Why change | Do not argue. Ask what worked and what did not. Offer a comparison on the points they care about and a reference call. | Reference developer (with consent, CUSTOMISE) |
| "What happens if you go out of business or sell the network?" | Long-term operator risk | Describe ownership, operating history, and the regulatory framework for wholesale access networks in plain terms. Do not speculate; refer to the agreement's continuity provisions. | Company overview; agreement clause reference [CONFIRM with legal] |
| "Our residents will complain to us when the internet is slow" | Support burden | Explain the support model: resident contacts their RSP; RSP escalates to the network operator; developer is not in the support chain; what the developer's sales team can hand buyers. | Resident welcome material; support flow diagram |
| "We need the comms room in the clubhouse which is Stage 4" | Programme mismatch | Explain the FDH and active equipment siting options and that the site must be available before Stage 1 RFS; offer alternatives (standalone cabinet, interim site). | Siting requirements (CUSTOMISE) |
| "Can you give us exclusivity / revenue share / marketing contribution?" | Commercial sweeteners | Note it, do not agree or refuse in the room; it goes to the commercial owner. Say what the standard agreement includes. | Standard agreement summary |
| "We are talking to <competitor> as well" | Leverage | Welcome the comparison; ask what criteria they will decide on; make sure our proposal answers those criteria explicitly. | Decision criteria list captured in the meeting |
| "Fibre is overkill for this estate" | Cost vs benefit | Explain that FTTP is now the standard greenfield expectation and that buyers and valuers ask; focus on move-in readiness and reliability rather than peak speed. | Market expectation evidence (CUSTOMISE) |

## Method

1. Listen for the real concern, restate it, confirm.
2. Answer with the response outline; keep to what the input and the CUSTOMISE defaults support.
3. Offer evidence; if it is not to hand, record an action to send it.
4. Ask a question that moves the conversation to the next decision.

## Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "NBN is terrible" or any competitor criticism | Unprofessional and unverifiable | "Here is what we do differently" |
| "We can definitely hit that date" | Unconfirmed commitment | "Based on our standard lead times, that date is achievable if civil handover is by <date>; we would confirm in the programme" |
| "It will cost you nothing" | Usually untrue once civil scope is counted | "Here is the scope split and what each party funds" |
| "Everyone uses us" | Overclaim | Name the estates (with consent) |
| "Trust me" | Nothing to evaluate | Offer the evidence or a reference |

## CUSTOMISE

- Delivered estates and reference developers the team is allowed to name.
- Current RSP list and onboarding lead time.
- Standard agreement summary: term, continuity provisions, what is and is not negotiable.
- Lead times used in the timing response.
- Who approves commercial deviations and how quickly they respond.
