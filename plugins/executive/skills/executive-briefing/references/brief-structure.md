# Brief structure and selection rules

A brief is one page. Its job is to let the executive walk into the meeting knowing what is working, what is not, what could go wrong and what only they can decide. Everything else is background and stays in the sources.

## Reading order for the sources

1. Skim every source for headings, RAG statuses, "decision", "approval", "risk", "escalate", "overdue", "variance" and numbers against plan. Build the structure map (document-reader) and the SOURCES list before writing anything.
2. Note the period each source covers; a brief that mixes August finance with September delivery must say so.
3. Record contradictions as you find them (two documents with different premises counts, a RAG green with a narrative that reads amber).

## What earns a place

| Test | Qualifies if | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | money, customers, premises, people, regulatory or contractual obligation at a scale the executive would act on | GOING WELL or REQUIRING ATTENTION |
| Urgency | a date inside the next period, or a deadline already missed | REQUIRING ATTENTION or DECISIONS REQUIRED |
| Only the executive can act | approval above a delegation, a cross-department conflict, a resource reallocation | DECISIONS REQUIRED |
| Could go wrong | a risk rated medium or higher by its source, or any risk without an owner | KEY RISKS |
| Will be asked about | a board, regulator, major RSP or developer topic the executive will face | whichever section fits |

Routine progress that is on plan does not qualify unless it is material (a milestone, a major estate live) or it closes a prior concern.

## Ranking inside a section

REQUIRING ATTENTION: deadline missed, then deadline inside the period, then largest impact, then items waiting on the executive.

GOING WELL: largest impact first, then items that resolve a prior concern, then milestones.

KEY RISKS: highest source rating first; ties broken by nearest trigger date, then by ownerless risks.

DECISIONS REQUIRED: earliest deadline first, then largest impact.

## Writing each point

Pattern: number, comparison, meaning, citation.

- "Opex $4.21m against $3.98m budget (+5.8%), driven by contractor splicing on two accelerated estates [S1 p.2]; expected to persist while the acceleration continues [S1 p.3]."
- "Riverbend stage 3 civil handover slipped from 12 Sep to 30 Oct [S3 §2.1]; 420 premises will not be connectable in Q4 [S3 §2.2]."
- "No owner is recorded for the power-dependency risk on Hillcrest [S4 row 9]."

Avoid: narrative build-up, restating the source's headings, more than one idea per point, adjectives without numbers.

## LIKELY IMPACT

Two or three sentences on what the period's picture means for the next period if nothing changes. Each sentence tied to a source. This is projection of the sources' own statements, not the briefing's forecast: "S2 states the shortfall will carry into Q4 unless power is energised by 15 Oct [S2 p.4]."

## NOT COVERED BY THE SOURCES

List what a reader would expect for the forum but no document addresses (cash position, safety incidents, customer complaints, RSP escalations, people matters). This stops the executive mistaking silence for good news.

## Length control

About 350 words for the body (sections GOING WELL to LIKELY IMPACT). If over: cut adjectives, merge points with the same cause, move detail to the decisions or risks outputs. If a section has fewer than three qualifying items, say so rather than padding.

## Period-on-period briefs

If the prior period's brief is supplied, add a one-line CHANGE SINCE LAST BRIEF at the top: resolved, new, still open. Cite both briefs.

## CUSTOMISE

Defaults to replace with the company's executive reporting conventions:

- Materiality for the brief: `$100,000` variance, `200` premises, any safety incident, any regulatory notice (verify)
- Forums and their readers: `Exec meeting (weekly, CEO and GMs)`, `Board (monthly)`, `Ops review (fortnightly)` (fill in)
- Common risk scale for consolidation: likelihood `Rare / Unlikely / Possible / Likely / Almost certain`; impact `Minor / Moderate / Major / Severe / Critical`; rating `Low / Medium / High / Extreme` (align to the enterprise risk framework)
- Delegation thresholds that turn an ask into an executive decision: `capex above $<x>`, `contract value above $<y>`, `headcount changes` (fill in)
- Standard sections the executive expects every period: `Safety, Customers and RSPs, Delivery and premises, Finance, People, Risk` (verify)
