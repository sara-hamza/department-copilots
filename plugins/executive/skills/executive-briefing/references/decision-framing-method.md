# Decision framing method

A decision is well framed when the executive can answer it in the meeting without asking for the paper to be rewritten. Frame every decision the same way so they can be compared.

## Step 1: find the decisions

Explicit: "seeks approval", "recommend that the executive", "decision required", "for endorsement", "request variation".

Implied: a blocked item whose unblock is a choice; two documents proposing different paths; a deadline that forces a choice between options; a risk whose mitigation needs funding or priority; an item escalated without a stated ask. Mark these "implied by [S<n>]" and phrase the question yourself, carefully, as the sources would.

Not decisions: information items, items already decided (cite where), items within someone else's delegation (say whose).

## Step 2: state the question

One sentence, answerable yes/no or by picking an option. "Should the company fund the Hillcrest temporary generator ($86,000) to hold the 30 Oct connection date, or accept the slip to December?" Not "Hillcrest power situation".

## Step 3: options

List the options the sources actually put forward, plus "do nothing" when it is a real path. For each: what it costs, what it gains, what it risks, as stated in the sources. If a source lists only one option, say "single option presented; alternatives not explored in sources".

| Option | Cost / effort | Benefit | Risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Fund generator | $86,000 opex | 420 premises connectable Q4 | generator supply lead time 3 weeks | [S3 §4] |
| B. Accept slip | nil now | avoids spend | 420 premises Dec; RSP commitments at risk | [S3 §4; S5 p.2] |
| C. Do nothing / defer | nil | time to negotiate with developer | date passes 15 Oct; option A no longer achievable | [S3 §4] |

## Step 4: trade-offs and reversibility

- Trade-offs: what is given up by each option, in the sources' words.
- Reversibility: a two-way door (can be undone cheaply: a trial, a deferral, a reversible policy) or a one-way door (contract signature, public commitment, capital committed, people decisions). One-way doors deserve more information before deciding; say what information.

## Step 5: recommendation basis

Report what the source recommends and the reasoning it gives. Do not add a recommendation of the briefing's own. If sources disagree, show both with citations. If no source recommends, write "no recommendation in sources" and list what would be needed to form one (a cost estimate, a legal view, a customer impact count).

## Step 6: deadline, consequence, owner

- Deadline: the date by which the decision must be made, and why that date (a contract expiry, a construction window, a board cycle). If the sources give none, write "no deadline stated" and, if a date drives it, name the driver.
- Consequence of missing it: which options fall away.
- Owner: who executes once decided, and who is accountable. Role, not name, unless the source uses the name for this purpose.

## Step 7: what is needed before deciding

Information the sources do not contain that the executive would reasonably ask for. Keep it short; this is the list the chief of staff chases before the meeting.

## Quality checks

- Could the executive decide from this block alone without opening the source? If not, the block is incomplete.
- Is every figure cited?
- Is "do nothing" shown where it is real?
- Is reversibility stated?
- Is the recommendation attributed to a source, not to the briefing?

## Worked example

```
DECISION 1: Fund a temporary generator at Hillcrest ($86,000) to hold the 30 Oct connection date, or accept the slip to December?
Raised by: Project delivery report, explicit request [S3 §4]
Deadline: 15 Oct 2026; generator lead time is 3 weeks [S3 §4.2]; if missed: option A no longer achievable, slip to December becomes the only path [S3 §4.2]
Owner: GM Delivery (execution); CEO (approval above $50,000 opex delegation) [S6 p.1]
Options:
  A. Fund generator : $86,000 opex, 420 premises connectable in Q4, supply risk on generator hire [S3 §4]
  B. Accept slip : no spend now; 420 premises move to December; two RSPs have marketed Q4 availability [S3 §4; S5 p.2]
  C. Defer decision past 15 Oct : equivalent to B [S3 §4.2]
Reversibility: A is a two-way door on spend (hire can be cancelled with 7 days notice [S3 §4.3]) but a one-way door on the date; B is one-way on the date.
Recommendation in sources: A, on the basis that RSP commitments and developer relationship outweigh the cost [S3 §4.4]; Finance notes the spend is unbudgeted [S1 p.6].
Needed before deciding: confirmation from the developer of the permanent power date [not in sources]; RSP commercial exposure if the date slips [S5 mentions but does not quantify].
```

## CUSTOMISE

- Delegation limits that define who decides: `<role>: opex $<x>, capex $<y>, contracts $<z>` (fill in)
- Standard decision forums and their lead times: `Exec weekly (papers 2 days prior)`, `Board monthly (papers 7 days prior)` (verify)
- One-way-door categories by policy: `contract execution, public announcements, headcount, capital above $<x>` (verify)
