# Sentiment and tone guide

Read sentiment from the customer's words, then choose a tone. The reply should lower the temperature without over-promising. Follow the writing-helper customer tone: warm, plain, accountable, no internal jargon.

## Reading sentiment

| Level | Signals in the text | What it means for the reply |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | Neutral description, asks a question, "just checking" | Standard tone; answer the question first |
| Frustrated | "Again", "still", "third time", "no one has", capital letters on a word or two | Acknowledge the repeat; be specific about what is different this time |
| Angry | Insults, all caps, "unacceptable", "disgrace", demands | Apology tone; short sentences; no defensiveness; one concrete next step with a time |
| Distressed | "Desperate", "I cannot work", medical references, crying mentioned in call notes | Prioritise; empathy first; check vulnerability signals; escalate internally |
| Threatening escalation | "Ombudsman", "lawyer", "media", "Facebook", "A Current Affair" | Firm-but-warm; acknowledge their right to escalate without arguing; set out exactly what we will do and when; flag internally |

Always quote the evidence. "Angry" without a quote is a guess.

## Tone selection

| Tone | Use when | Opening line pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Calm or mildly frustrated; first contact | "Thanks for getting in touch about <issue>." |
| Apology | Repeat contact, missed appointment, our error, angry customer | "I am sorry this has taken longer than it should have, and thank you for your patience while we <action>." |
| Firm | Customer is demanding something we cannot do (credits, speaking for the RSP, a date we do not have) | "I understand why you are asking for <x>. Here is what we can do, and what sits with your provider." |

## De-escalation phrases that work

- "You are right that this has gone on too long."
- "Here is exactly what has happened so far and what happens next."
- "I have checked this myself today."
- "The next update will come from us by <day/time>, whether or not there is news."
- "If that time passes and you have not heard from us, please reply to this message."
- "Your provider manages that part of the service, and I have noted what you told me so you do not have to repeat it." (only if that note was actually made)

## Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Unfortunately" as the first word | Signals bad news before the content | Lead with the fact |
| "As per our previous email" | Reads as blame | "As we mentioned on <date>" |
| "There is nothing we can do" | Closes the conversation | "What we can do is <x>; <y> is handled by your provider" |
| "This is an RSP issue" | Jargon and blame | "Your provider looks after this part of the service" |
| "The fault is with your modem" | Unconfirmed and blames the customer | "The next thing to check is the equipment in your home, which your provider can help with" |
| "Should be fixed by tomorrow" | Promise not in our control | "Our technician is booked for <window>" or "we will update you by <time>" |
| "Our mistake" or "our fault" | Liability wording; only if the employee decides to use it | "We found <fact> and we are fixing it" |
| "Please be patient" | Dismissive | "Thank you for your patience while we <specific action>" |

## What not to promise

| Do not promise | Because | What you can say |
|---|---|---|
| A fix date or time without a confirmed booking | Field capacity and fault cause are not confirmed | "Our technician is booked for <window>" or "we will update you by <time>" |
| A credit, refund or compensation | Billing is with the RSP; compensation follows a process (CUSTOMISE) | "Any billing adjustment is handled by your provider" |
| That the problem is the RSP's | Not confirmed and not our place | "We have checked our part of the connection and found <fact>; the next step is with your provider" |
| A specific speed | Speed depends on plan, modem and Wi-Fi | "The connection to your home is capable of the speed your plan provides" (only if confirmed by NOC) |
| That a technician will not need access inside | Often they do | "The technician may need access to the connection box inside the home" |
| Confidentiality of what they told us from the RSP | We may need to share to fix it | "We will share only what is needed to fix the connection" |

## Structure for every reply (writing-helper)

1. Acknowledge: what they told us, in their terms. Thank them for specifics.
2. What we found: plain English, only facts in the input, "appears" for anything unconfirmed.
3. What happens next and when: one next step; a time for the next update.
4. How to reach us: contact line (CUSTOMISE) and, where relevant, that their provider is the right contact for plan, billing or modem questions.

Channel limits: email full structure; SMS under 300 characters with one next step and a reference; portal same as email without the sign-off block.

## CUSTOMISE

- Sign-off name and team ("Customer Operations, <company>" by default).
- Contact line: phone, hours, email, reference number format.
- Compensation and credit policy wording approved by compliance.
- Final-response wording that references the external complaints body, if required.
- Approved apology wording for missed appointments.
